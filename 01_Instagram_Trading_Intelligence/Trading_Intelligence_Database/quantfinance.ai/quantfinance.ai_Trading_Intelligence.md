- **Format:** `Video / Reel`

#### Written Technical Rules & Analysis:

> 1️⃣ Bid-Ask Spread → the price of immediacy. Market orders pay half the spread.
>
> 2️⃣ Order Book Depth → how much size rests at each level. Thin book = big moves.
>
> 3️⃣ Market Impact → square-root law: ΔP ≈ Yσ√(Q/V). 4× size, only 2× impact.
>
> 4️⃣ Adverse Selection → spreads widen to survive informed traders (Glosten–Milgrom, 1985).
>
> 5️⃣ Queue Position → price-time priority. Same price, earlier order, filled first.
>
> 6️⃣ Latency Arbitrage → fast traders snipe stale quotes (Budish, Cramton & Shim, 2015).
>
> 7️⃣ Maker-Taker Fees → exchanges pay you to post, charge you to take. US cap: 30 mils/share.
>
> 8️⃣ Slippage → the gap between decision price and fill (Perold's implementation shortfall).
>
> Alpha is what you find. Microstructure is what you keep. 💡

### Setup #05: Your portfolio is "diversified"... until the market crashes. 📉

- **Target Asset / Market:** `Stocks`
- **Trading Domain:** `Options Trading, Quantitative & Algorithmic, Risk Management`
- **Key Indicators & Tools:** `Price Action & Market Structure`
- **Format:** `Video / Reel`

#### Written Technical Rules & Analysis:

> Your portfolio is "diversified"... until the market crashes. 📉
>
> Normal days: each sector moves on its own.
>
> Average ρ ≈ 0.2 → portfolio risk 12%
>
> Crisis: they all fall together.
>
> Average ρ → 0.9 → portfolio risk 19%
>
> Same portfolio. Same weights. 60% more risk.
>
> σₚ = σ√(1/n + (1−1/n)ρ̄)
>
> Why does this happen? 🧠
>
> In a one-factor model, ρᵢⱼ ≈ βᵢβⱼσ²ₘ/(σᵢσⱼ).
>
> When market volatility σₘ spikes, the common factor swamps each sector's own risk, so correlations rise mechanically.
>
> For the quants 🤓
>
> • Correlation measured in high-vol regimes is biased upward (Forbes & Rigobon, 2002)
>
> • Real crises spike σ too, so actual risk rises even more than shown
>
> • Hedges cost money: puts bleed premium in calm markets
>
> We saw it in 2008. We saw it in March 2020.
>
> Diversify for normal days. Hedge for crisis days. 🛡️
>

### Setup #06: Most people think gamma scalping is "buy low, sell high."

- **Target Asset / Market:** `Options`
- **Trading Domain:** `Scalping, Options Trading, Quantitative & Algorithmic`
- **Key Indicators & Tools:** `Price Action & Market Structure`
- **Format:** `Video / Reel`

#### Written Technical Rules & Analysis:

> Most people think gamma scalping is "buy low, sell high."
>
> Quants know it's a bet on one thing: realized vol vs implied vol. ⚡
>
> 1️⃣ Θ = −½ΓS²σᵢ² → theta IS the price of gamma (16% vol ≈ 1% daily breakeven)
>
> 2️⃣ Daily P&L ≈ ½ΓS²[(ΔS/S)² − σᵢ²Δt]
>
> 3️⃣ Path dependence → vol near the strike pays most (dollar gamma weighting)
>
> 4️⃣ Hedge at implied vs realized vol → smooth path or locked total (Ahmad–Wilmott)
>
> 5️⃣ Discrete hedging error ∝ 1/√N (Derman–Kamal)
>
> 6️⃣ With costs, hedge in a band, not at a point (Whalley–Wilmott)
>
> 7️⃣ IV > RV on average → long gamma is negative carry
>
> 8️⃣ BS delta ignores the smile → use skew-aware delta
>
> The edge isn't the hedge. It's forecasting realized vol better than the market. 📈
