#### Written Technical Rules & Analysis:

> What happens when fundamental investing meets quantitative research?
>
> Quantamental investing combines financial analysis with systematic, data-driven methods to identify investment opportunities.
>
> A fundamental analyst might study a company’s cash flows, margins, balance sheet and management. A quantitative researcher might turn those same variables into measurable signals, test them across thousands of stocks, and evaluate whether they hold up out of sample.
>
> The interesting part is how the two approaches complement each other.
>
> In this breakdown, we explore:
>
> → Fundamental analysis vs. quantitative investing
>
> → How financial statement data becomes an investment signal
>
> → The Information Ratio and breadth of independent bets
>
> → Piotroski’s F-Score and systematic stock selection
>
> → Alternative data and NLP in financial research
>
> → Portfolio construction, position sizing and correlated exposures
>
> → Backtest selection bias and the limits of historical performance
>
> The goal isn’t simply to automate stock picking. It’s to combine sound financial reasoning with reproducible research, disciplined portfolio construction and rigorous out-of-sample testing.
>
> For anyone exploring quant finance, quantitative research, systematic investing or quantamental strategies, understanding both sides is a useful place to start.
