#### Written Technical Rules & Analysis:

> IRL + ERL — Understanding the relationship between internal and external liquidity to anticipate potential price movements. Internal Range Liquidity (IRL) refers to liquidity within a defined trading range, often associated with Fair Value Gaps (FVGs) and internal swing points. External Range Liquidity (ERL) refers to liquidity beyond the range's boundaries, typically resting above significant highs and below significant lows. Price can move between these liquidity levels, sweeping external liquidity before returning to internal liquidity, or moving from internal liquidity toward external targets. Understanding this relationship helps traders identify potential liquidity draws, retracements, and continuation opportunities. The key is to analyze the overall range, market structure, and price delivery rather than assuming price must follow a fixed sequence.

> CRT (Candle Range Theory) — Understanding how price manipulates a range before delivering toward its opposite side. - **Trading Domain:** `Options Trading, Order Flow & Volume Profile, Smart Money Concepts (SMC)`
- **Key Indicators & Tools:** `Price Action & Market Structure`
- **Format:** `Single Photo`

#### Written Technical Rules & Analysis:

> Candle Chart vs. Cluster Chart: A candlestick chart displays price movement through the open, high, low, and close of each candle, helping traders analyze market structure, trends, and price-action patterns. A cluster chart, also known as a footprint chart, provides more granular information by displaying buying and selling volume at individual price levels within each candle. While candlestick charts help identify BOS, CHoCH, liquidity sweeps, and key points of interest, cluster charts reveal order-flow details such as bid-ask imbalances, absorption, and aggressive buying or selling. Traders can use candlestick charts to establish directional context and cluster charts to examine how buyers and sellers interact at critical levels. Combining both can provide a more comprehensive view of the market, although neither chart type guarantees accurate predictions.

### Setup #11: FVG (Fair Value Gap) is a three-candle imbalance that forms when 

- **Target Asset / Market:** `Equities & Financial Markets`
- **Trading Domain:** `Smart Money Concepts (SMC)`
- **Key Indicators & Tools:** `FVG`
- **Format:** `Carousel (Sidecar)`

#### Written Technical Rules & Analysis:

> FVG (Fair Value Gap) is a three-candle imbalance that forms when price moves aggressively, leaving an area with limited overlap between the first and third candles. It represents potential inefficiency in price delivery, where price may later return to rebalance the gap before continuing its move. A bullish FVG forms during strong upward displacement, while a bearish FVG forms during strong downward displacement. IFVG (Inverse Fair Value Gap) occurs when an existing FVG is decisively violated and subsequently acts as an opposing zone—for example, a bullish FVG may become resistance after price breaks below it. Traders use FVGs and IFVGs alongside liquidity sweeps, market structure shifts, and higher-timeframe bias to identify potential entry zones, rather than treating every gap as a guaranteed reaction area.

- **Target Asset / Market:** `Equities & Financial Markets`
- **Trading Domain:** `Options Trading, Smart Money Concepts (SMC)`
- **Key Indicators & Tools:** `Price Action & Market Structure`
- **Format:** `Carousel (Sidecar)`

#### Written Technical Rules & Analysis:

> CISD (Change in State of Delivery) is a price-action concept that identifies a potential shift in the market’s delivery from bullish to bearish or bearish to bullish. It occurs when price decisively moves through a significant candle or sequence of candles associated with the previous directional delivery, suggesting that control may be shifting between buyers and sellers. A bearish CISD indicates potential selling pressure after bullish delivery, while a bullish CISD suggests potential buying pressure after bearish delivery. Traders often look for CISD following a liquidity sweep and around key points of interest (POIs) to strengthen their entry confirmation. However, a CISD alone does not guarantee a reversal and should be evaluated alongside market structure, displacement, and higher-timeframe bias.
